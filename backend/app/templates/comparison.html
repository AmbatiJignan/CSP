Comparative Analysis

Organic vs. Conventional Farming

An objective agronomic comparison of inputs, soil biology, economics, and long-term sustainability.

Contextual Note: Agricultural performance varies markedly by crop, climatic zone, water availability, and management competence. This table reflects general systemic characteristics rather than rigid universal assertions.
Dimension Organic Farming Systems Conventional Farming Systems
Farming Inputs Biological & On-Farm: Composts, vermicompost, cow dung, neem formulations, bio-fertilisers, green manures. Minimal off-farm synthetic purchases. Manufactured & Commercial: Synthetic water-soluble fertilisers (Urea, DAP, MOP), hybrid seeds, systemic chemical pesticides and fungicides.
Soil Management Living Ecosystem Focus: Prioritises building soil organic carbon, mycorrhizal fungal networks, aggregate structure, and earthworm biodiversity. Nutrient Delivery Focus: Directly feeds crop roots with soluble salts; native soil biological cycles may decline if organic manure is neglected.
Pest & Disease Control Prevention & Ecological Balance: Crop rotation, border trap crops, yellow sticky traps, botanical repellents (Neemastra), and conservation of beneficial predators. Targeted Eradication: Chemical sprays (organophosphates, synthetic pyrethroids). Rapid knockdown effect, but risks developing pest resistance over time.
Fertiliser Usage Slow-Release Organics: Nutrients mineralise gradually matching plant uptake curves. Zero chemical nitrate leaching into groundwater aquifers. Fast-Acting Solubles: Immediate crop greening and rapid vegetative response. High vulnerability to leaching and volatilisation losses (up to 50%).
Biodiversity Impact High Ecosystem Support: Protects pollinators (bees, butterflies), soil macrofauna, predatory spiders, birds, and aquatic life in surrounding water bodies. Potential Ecological Disruption: Broad-spectrum sprays may unintentionally harm non-target pollinators and beneficial parasitic insects.
Water Efficiency High Retention: Soils rich in organic humus retain up to 25%–30% more moisture, imparting drought resilience during monsoon dry spells. Standard Moisture Retention: Soils with depleted organic matter tend to form crusts, suffering increased runoff and requiring more frequent irrigation.
Cost & Working Capital Low Cash Outlay, Higher Labor: Drastically cuts cash spent at agrochemical dealers. Requires manual labor for weeding and on-farm input preparation. High Cash Outlay, Lower Labor: Chemical weedicides and fertilisers reduce manual hours, but expose smallholders to substantial seasonal input costs and credit risks.
Yield Dynamics Gradual Stability: May experience a 10%–25% yield dip during initial 2–3 transition years while soil biology recovers; stabilises and builds resilience over time. High Immediate Yields: Strong initial crop response and high yields under favorable rainfall and input management; potential plateauing without organic matter.
Long-Term Sustainability High Generational Endurance: Continuously rebuilds topsoil fertility, sequesters carbon, and protects community water tables for decades. Requires Strict Management: High yields can be sustained long-term only if paired with integrated nutrient management and strict soil conservation.
