Understanding Organic Farming

Principles, objectives, and ecological foundations of sustainable agriculture.

What is Organic Farming?

Organic farming is a comprehensive holistic production management system that promotes and enhances agro-ecosystem health, including biodiversity, biological cycles, and soil biological activity. It emphasizes the use of management practices in preference to the use of off-farm inputs, taking into account that regional conditions require locally adapted systems.

Unlike production models that rely on synthetically manufactured chemical fertilisers, synthetic chemical pesticides, and genetically modified organisms, organic agriculture prioritises cultural, biological, and mechanical methods to foster nutrient cycling, conserve water, and sustain soil fertility over generations.

The Four Core Principles

Formulated by the International Federation of Organic Agriculture Movements (IFOAM), these four universal principles inspire all organic systems:

1. Principle of Health

Organic agriculture should sustain and enhance the health of soil, plant, animal, human and planet as one and indivisible. Healthy soils yield healthy crops that nourish humans.

2. Principle of Ecology

Organic agriculture should be based on living ecological systems and cycles, work with them, emulate them and help sustain them through local ecological adaptation.

3. Principle of Fairness

Organic agriculture should build on relationships that ensure fairness with regard to the common environment and life opportunities, providing farmers with equitable living conditions.

4. Principle of Care

Organic agriculture should be managed in a precautionary and responsible manner to protect the health and well-being of current and future generations and the environment.

Primary Objectives

  • Maintain High Soil Biological Activity: Stimulating beneficial microbes, mycorrhizal fungi, and earthworms that liberate natural soil nutrients.
  • Close Nutrient Loops on the Farm: Returning composted crop residues, animal manures, and green manures back to cultivated plots.
  • Conserve Water & Reduce Runoff: Enhancing organic matter so soils behave like sponges, retaining precious monsoon precipitation.
  • Preserve Biodiversity & Pollinators: Providing non-toxic habitats for bees, butterflies, predatory beetles, and soil fauna.
  • Reduce Farmer Input Costs: Eliminating reliance on expensive commercial synthetic pesticides and fertilisers.
