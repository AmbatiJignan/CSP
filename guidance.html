Practical Farmer Guidance Manual

Practical recommendations for transitioning land, preparing soils, and managing crops organically.

Local Advisory Notice

Agricultural practices are site-specific. The guidance presented below represents general best practices. Farmers are strongly encouraged to adapt schedules, input dosages, and crop varieties to their specific soil texture, irrigation status, and advice from local Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVK) and Agricultural Extension Officers.

1
Phased Transition Strategy (Do Not Switch Overnight)

Abruptly withdrawing all synthetic fertilisers often causes an alarming yield reduction because chemical-treated soils temporarily lack sufficient active beneficial bacteria.

  • Year 1: Transition 25%–33% of your plot. Reduce synthetic fertiliser by 30% while doubling FYM and applying Azospirillum/PSB.
  • Year 2: Grow a green manure crop (Sunnhemp/Dhaincha). Reduce synthetic fertiliser by 60%. Observe soil crumb structure.
  • Year 3: Full organic production with on-farm vermicompost, Jeevamrutham, and bio-control traps.
2
Soil Preparation & Biological Inoculation

Proper land tillage prepares the seedbed and activates native microbes:

  • Deep summer ploughing exposes resting soil pupae, nematode cysts, and weed roots to solar sterilisation.
  • Incorporate 4 to 5 tonnes of well-rotted FYM per acre 3 to 4 weeks prior to sowing.
  • Apply bio-fertiliser mixtures (Rhizobium for legumes, PSB for phosphorus release) mixed with moist compost directly into planting furrows.
3
On-Farm Liquid Microbial Inoculants (Jeevamrutham)

A traditional, low-cost bio-stimulant that introduces trillions of beneficial microorganisms:

  • Recipe for 1 Acre: Mix 10 kg fresh indigenous cow dung, 5–10 litres cow urine, 2 kg jaggery, 2 kg pulse flour (besan), and a handful of virgin forest or field-bund soil into 200 litres water.
  • Stir clockwise twice daily under shade for 48–72 hours.
  • Apply through irrigation channels or spray diluted (10%) onto crop foliage at 21-day intervals.
4
Proactive & Preventive Pest Defense

In organic agriculture, pest control is preventative rather than retaliatory:

  • Install 5 yellow sticky traps and 5 pheromone traps per acre early in the vegetative phase to monitor initial moth flights.
  • Plant African marigold along plot borders to trap fruit borer moths.
  • At first sighting of minor sucking pests, spray 5% Neem Seed Kernel Extract (NSKE) or 10,000 ppm cold-pressed neem oil (3 ml/litre).
5
Moisture Conservation & Mulching

Retaining moisture protects crops from intense summer heatwaves:

  • Maintain continuous organic cover: never leave topsoil naked beneath scorching sun.
  • Mulch row crops with dried straw or sugarcane trash at 2–3 tonnes/acre.
  • Adopt alternate furrow irrigation or broad bed furrow (BBF) systems to conserve 30% of irrigation water.
6
Seed Treatment & Organic Certification

Strengthening seedling vigour from the very start:

  • Treat seeds with Trichoderma viride (4g/kg seed) or Pseudomonas fluorescens (10g/kg seed) to prevent damping off and fungal root rot.
  • Maintain transparent farm record diaries (sowing dates, input batches, harvest tallies) required for Participatory Guarantee System (PGS-India) certification.
