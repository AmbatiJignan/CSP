Technique #1

Aerobic Farm Composting

Introduction

Composting is the biological decomposition of organic waste materials into a rich, dark soil amendment known as humus. It transforms bulky crop residues, vegetable trimmings, dry leaves, and cattle dung into stable plant-assimilable nutrition.

How It Works

Thermophilic bacteria and fungi break down carbon-rich brown material and nitrogen-rich green material under oxygenated, moist conditions, generating internal heat up to 55°C–65°C that neutralizes weed seeds and pathogens.

Materials Required
  • Browns: Dry leaves, straw, shredded stalks (Carbon source).
  • Greens: Cattle dung, fresh weeds, green waste (Nitrogen source).
  • Water and fertile farm topsoil (microbial inoculant).
Basic Procedure
  1. Select an elevated, well-shaded plot (10 ft × 5 ft × 3 ft).
  2. Lay a bottom 6-inch layer of coarse dry twigs and stalks for aeration.
  3. Alternate layers of brown material (4 parts) and green manure / cow dung slurry (1 part).
  4. Maintain moisture at roughly 50%–60% (damp sponge consistency).
  5. Turn the pile every 15 to 20 days to re-introduce oxygen. Mature in 60 to 90 days.
Advantages: Restores soil microbial life, enhances organic carbon, recycles farm debris on-site at near zero financial cost.
Precautions: Never allow water-logging (causes anaerobic stench); avoid diseased crop material or perennial rhizomatous weeds.
Technique #2

Vermicomposting

Introduction

Vermicomposting uses specialised epigeic earthworm species (notably Eisenia fetida or Eudrilus eugeniae) to consume semi-decomposed organic wastes and excrete nutrient-dense vermicast.

How It Works

Earthworms consume organic residues, passing them through their digestive tract where enzymes and symbiotic gut bacteria enrich the cast with gibberellins, auxins, and micronutrients.

Materials Required
  • Earthworms (1,000–1,500 per cubic metre).
  • Semi-digested cattle dung (cool, 10–15 days aged).
  • Shredded crop straw, bagasse, banana pseudo-stems.
  • Thatched shaded shed or vermi-bed (HDPE / brick).
Basic Procedure
  1. Construct a bed under permanent shade with a vermiwash collection spout.
  2. Place a 3-inch bedding layer of coconut coir or straw.
  3. Add aged cattle dung mixed with chopped biomass in 6-inch increments.
  4. Introduce earthworms gently across the top surface.
  5. Sprinkle water daily to preserve 65% moisture. Harvest black granular castings after 45–60 days.
Advantages: Up to 5x higher nitrogen and 7x higher potassium than ordinary compost; produces liquid vermiwash as foliar tonic.
Precautions: Guard against ants, frogs, and rodents; never use hot fresh cow dung (fermentation heat kills worms).
Technique #3

Farmyard Manure (FYM) Preparation

Introduction

Farmyard Manure refers to the decomposed mixture of cattle dung, cattle urine, litter, and left-over fodder. It is the cornerstone of traditional Indian rural agriculture.

How It Works

Proper pit-curing prevents volatilisation of valuable ammoniacal nitrogen from animal urine and retains potassium in manure aggregates.

Materials Required
  • Cattle dung and urine absorbed in straw beddings.
  • Pit with shade (6 ft wide × 3 ft deep × length according to herd size).
Basic Procedure
  1. Collect daily cattle barn sweepings including urine-soaked bedding.
  2. Fill the pit from one end, compacting slightly and covering with a thin layer of soil.
  3. Once filled to 1.5 ft above ground, dome with mud plaster to exclude rainwater.
  4. Allow 4 to 5 months for anaerobic curing into rich crumbly manure.
Advantages: Enriches both major nutrients and trace minerals; improves soil physical tilth and water infiltration.
Precautions: Never heap uncovered manure in open fields where sunlight destroys nitrogen and rains leach nutrients.
Technique #4

Green Manure & Green Leaf Manuring

Introduction

Green manuring is the practice of growing fast-growing leguminous crops and incorporating them into the soil at flowering stage to supply organic matter and atmospheric nitrogen.

How It Works

Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules fix atmospheric nitrogen. Incorporating succulent vegetative tissues before lignification allows rapid decomposition within 10–14 days.

Suitable Green Crops
  • Sunnhemp (Crotalaria juncea)
  • Dhaincha (Sesbania aculeata) - highly salt tolerant
  • Cowpea, Cluster bean, or Green leaf loppings (Gliricidia, Pongamia).
Basic Procedure
  1. Broadcast seeds with early pre-monsoon showers at 20–25 kg/acre.
  2. Grow for 45 to 55 days until flowering begins (peak biomass and nitrogen).
  3. Plough down into soil with a disc plough or rotavator.
  4. Allow 10 to 14 days with adequate soil moisture for microbial breakdown before sowing the main crop.
Advantages: Contributes 40–80 kg biological nitrogen per hectare; suppresses weeds and loosens hard soil pans.
Precautions: Do not delay incorporation past pod initiation (woody stalks rob soil nitrogen during decomposition).

5. Crop Rotation & Diversification

Alternating heavy feeding crops (e.g., maize, paddy) with nitrogen-fixing legumes (e.g., groundnut, red gram) and deep-rooted taproot species breaks pest life cycles, prevents soil nutrient depletion at identical root depths, and minimizes market risk.

6. Biofertilisers & Microbial Inoculants

Biofertilisers contain living strains of beneficial microorganisms: Rhizobium (for pulse seed treatment), Azospirillum & Azotobacter (free-living nitrogen fixers for cereals), Phosphobacteria (PSB) (solubilises locked soil phosphorus), and VAM (Mycorrhizae) (massively expands root surface area for moisture and zinc uptake).

7. Organic Mulching

Covering bare soil around crop root zones with crop residues, dried grass, or sugarcane bagasse reduces soil temperature by 3°C–5°C in summer, suppresses weed germination, prevents splash erosion, and decomposes gradually into topsoil humus.

8. Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

A multi-tiered ecological defense strategy: Yellow sticky cards (for aphids and whiteflies), blue sticky cards (for thrips), pheromone traps (for bollworms and fruit borers), border crops of marigold (trap crop for caterpillars) and castor, paired with calibrated sprays of 5% Neem Seed Kernel Extract (NSKE).

9. Biological Pest Control

Using living beneficial organisms: Releasing Trichogramma egg parasitoid wasps to control lepidopteran larvae, Chrysoperla carnea (green lacewing) larvae to voraciously consume sucking pests, and applying bio-pesticides such as Beauveria bassiana and Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt).
